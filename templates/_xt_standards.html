Evidence & assumptions

How a shift is replayed. For each past shift with volume: the veterans who were in (booked any hours), at the veteran rate for the day's shift length, plus the cross-trainees the standard would have provided — its target times the share of their department that was in that day, never more than their department could spare after its own volume. The shift meets plan if that covers the planned units. Coverage is the share that do; the recommended target is the smallest that reaches {{ bar_pct }}%, and a decrease never goes below the most cross-trainees actually used on one shift.

Switch is proposed when another department reaches the bar with fewer people trained. Hire when no target does, because the sources cannot spare enough people on enough shifts.

Staffing policies decide who may staff a process at all: a forbidden source counts for nobody, and a home-team-only process gets Home team only — remove the cross-training standard, and hire if the home team is short. Seasons: judged during one, only its days are replayed; outside, its days are left out. During a protected season, or in the {{ v.settings.protect_weeks }} weeks before it, a cut becomes Hold. Training costs {{ v.settings.default_train_hours | round | int }} hours a person unless a process has its own figure; a switch must train fewer people.

Rates are each group's units over hours on the process across the window. A department with no history on a process takes the cross-trainee average, and the evidence says so. Absence is measured as scheduled (shift calendar) with no hours booked, where there are at least 20 scheduled person-days to measure; otherwise {{ v.settings.absence_pct }}%.

Not modelled: which individuals moved, learning curves, and stoppages that headcount could not have fixed — the bar sitting below 100% is what absorbs those.
